Is Sunny San Diego Really Stable?

San Diego is often imagined as warm, sunny, and predictable. But climate data shows that stability depends on what we measure: temperature extremes, coastal ocean conditions, precipitation, and sea level all tell different parts of the story.

California is not one climate.

Coastal, inland, mountain, and desert regions show very different temperature ranges.

Extremes matter more than averages.

A stable mean can hide hotter highs, warmer lows, or widening temperature ranges.The average might still stay the same but is the extremes becoming more extreme? Is the weather reaching new highs or new lows. This graph explore those extreme temperatures.

Now look closer at each region of California

The statewide view gives context, but each area has its own changes. Whether or not the region is close to the shore, or in the mountains could affect the change in extreme temperatures over the years. Some areas might change dramatically more than others.

What the patterns reveal

1. San Diego is stable only in reputation.

The data shows year-to-year swings in coastal temperature, sea level height, and precipitation.

2. Temperature extremes create the larger climate context.

Project 3 shows that San Diego sits within a broader California pattern of uneven climate change.

3. The next layer is regional comparison.

Future versions could compare coastal, inland, mountain, and desert San Diego regions, then connect rainfall and vegetation patterns to ecosystem stress.
